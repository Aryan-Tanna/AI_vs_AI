RULES OF DECISION
1. Decide only on the record, the statutes and the precedents shown below. Do not rely on anything else you may know about this matter, the parties, or how any court actually decided it. If the matter seems familiar, set that aside.
2. Every fact you rely on must be a record item shown below, cited by its ID in square brackets (F = stipulated fact, C = contested fact, EX = exhibit, AM = amount). 3. Every rule you apply must be a statute ID or a precedent ID shown below. Cite nothing else.
4. The two sides' submissions are presented as blocks. The order of the blocks is arbitrary and carries no weight. Length carries no weight: judge what was argued, not how much.
5. A VERIFIER FLAG marks a provable error that counsel did not correct. Take it into account as a fact about that submission; do not penalise it more than once.

WHAT TO DECIDE
For each framed issue ($issue_ids), and for no other issue, give:
- issue_id;
- finding: your holding on the issue in one or two sentences;
- governing_rule_ids: the statute IDs and/or precedent IDs the finding rests on (at least one);
- record_ids: the record IDs the rule is applied to (may be empty only for a pure question of law);
- application: how the rule applies to those record items;
- conclusion: what follows on this issue;
- upholds: PETITIONER if the finding upholds Petitioner's counsel's position on this issue, RESPONDENT if it upholds Respondent's counsel's position, NEITHER if it upholds neither.

Then give overall_result (PETITIONER or RESPONDENT: which side prevails on the reliefs sought) and overall_reasons. The overall result must follow from your issue findings.

Finally score the advocacy of each side on each issue you decided, each dimension from 0 to 1:
- accuracy: law and record stated correctly;
- consistency: no contradiction across that side's own turns;
- rebuttal: the other side's points on the issue were answered, not ignored or merely restated;
- grounding: claims tied to record, statute or precedent IDs.
The scores are a secondary measure of advocacy. They do not decide the issues.
